# song-zipf-test

A pre-registered test by MV2 of Munim, Inc. of a widely reported claim: that humpback whale
song (Arnon et al. 2025, *Science*) and Bengalese finch song (Kirby et al. 2026, *Science
Advances*) contain "language-like" units whose frequencies follow Zipf's law.

**Question.** Both papers cut songs into chunks at dips in transitional probability and
find that the chunk frequencies look Zipfian. The finch paper shows that the effect beats a
first-order (bigram) null. Is it more than **second-order syllable rules**? We test the
published statistic against pooled first- and second-order Markov chains fitted to each
recording, and add a held-out check against overfitting.

## First result (2026-11-17): the published controls are passed by song with no words

[RESULTS-CALIB.md](RESULTS-CALIB.md). In a pre-registered synthetic calibration at the size
of the finch data, song generated by first-order syllable-transition rules **with no words
at all** gave a mean Zipf R^2 of 0.85 (finch: 0.89) and beat **both** the shuffle and the
rotation control in 20 of 20 replicates. Every source we tried passed both controls,
including second-order chains and word lexicons. These two controls are the only ones
reported for the humpback whale result. The finch paper's extra bigram control is the
informative one. Our own second-order test separated clean word-like song from Markov song
(40 of 40 and 0 of 40), but lost most of its power when 10% of syllables were noisy (2 of 20).
Registered predictions: 6 of 7 came true; Brier score 0.173.

Next: a whale-sized calibration and a power-versus-noise curve, registered in
[CALIB-2-PREREG.md](CALIB-2-PREREG.md) before it runs.

## Code

* `zipfseg.py`: segmentation, the Zipf R^2, and the null models (pure Python).
* `calib.py`, `calib2.py`: the synthetic calibrations.
* `grade_final.py`: the final reading of the real-data results (no new statistics).
* `finch_parse.py`, `ocr_finch.py`, `job_analyze.py`, `inventory.py`: data stages.
* Tests: `python -m unittest -v test_zipfseg test_finch_parse test_calib test_grade_final
  test_calib_results` (`test_calib2` needs `calib.py` and `zipfseg.py` beside it).

The "wow", whichever way it falls: either the Zipf structure in animal song reduces to
short-range transition rules, or it survives a much stronger control than any published one.

Raw data from the two papers are not redistributed here. The finch data are CC BY-NC, and
the whale data are released for reproducing or extending the Zipfian analysis.
