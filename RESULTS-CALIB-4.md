## Headline

Arnon et al. 2025 (*Science* 387:649) report that the chunks their method finds in eight
years of humpback whale song obey **Zipf's law of brevity** (frequent chunks are shorter):
R^2 = 0.62, far above 1000 shuffled datasets (z = 37.57) and 1000 rotated datasets
(z = 13.92). Those two baselines are the only controls reported for it.

We ran the same statistic at whale size (8 "years" of 5,000-8,000 elements each) on
synthetic song whose truth we know. **Song generated by syllable-to-syllable rules, with no
words in it at all, passed both controls in 20 of 20 replicates, in all three wordless
scenarios** (shuffle z 28 to 86, rotation z 10 to 14; the whale values are 37.6 and 13.9).

Rule BW1 therefore reads `WORDLESS_SONG_PASSES_WHALE_BREVITY_CONTROLS`. As registered, we
say: the whale paper's two brevity controls are passed by song made from syllable-transition
rules with no words, so on their own they are not evidence of whale-relevant units.

**BW2 also holds.** A word source whose true words are *longer when more frequent* (slope
positive in 20 of 20 replicates, mean +0.28) was reported as brevity-following in 20 of 20
replicates and passed both whale controls in 18 of 20.

## What does carry information: the absolute R^2 and the Markov nulls

* **The R^2 level is not reproduced.** Wordless song gave mean R^2 of 0.09 to 0.17. Even
  clean word sources gave only 0.21 to 0.45. The whale value, 0.62, is higher than every
  source here (Q4 false). The controls cannot tell words from no words, but the size of the
  whale R^2 is not something these generators produce. The real whale test has to explain
  it.
* **The pooled Markov nulls behave as a test should.** On wordless first-order song, the
  first-order null was beaten in 0 of 20 replicates, which is the right size (BW3 holds). On
  word sources, the second-order null was beaten in 20 of 20 (equal weights) and 19 of 20
  (brevity weights), so it has power (Q11 true). For the anti-brevity source it was beaten in
  only 5 of 20. So a Markov null detects that there are units, but not which way their
  length relation runs. The real whale brevity test therefore uses the pooled first- and
  second-order nulls as its deciding baselines, as registered.

## Rules fixed in advance, applied

* Sanity: the brevity lexicon's true words have a negative slope in 20 of 20 replicates and
  the anti-brevity lexicon's a positive slope in 20 of 20. Both scenarios are valid.
* **BW1:** `WORDLESS_SONG_PASSES_WHALE_BREVITY_CONTROLS` (all three wordless scenarios).
  This was registered as the headline either way.
* **BW2 holds:** the pipeline reports brevity, with both controls passed, when the true
  units show the opposite relation.
* **BW3 holds:** the pooled first-order null has the right size for this statistic. The real
  whale brevity test uses the pooled first- and second-order nulls as its deciding baselines.
* **BW4 does not hold:** the published controls do not separate wordless from word-like song.

## What this does NOT show

* It says nothing about whether whale song has units. It says that the two published brevity
  controls cannot answer that question, and that the whale R^2 of 0.62 is higher than any
  synthetic source here, wordless or not. That is the next thing to explain.
* One generator family: alphabets of 12 or 24, Dirichlet transition rows, 12-word lexicons of
  length 3-7, no noise, and a fresh source every year. Real whale song changes gradually from
  year to year and shares material between years.
* B = 100 instead of the paper's 1000. That limits the p resolution to 0.0099; the z scores
  are comparable.
* The authors' Code S1 (read after this ran) computes brevity as a Poisson mixed-model slope
  (length coefficient -0.120, z = -29.5, 3,386 chunk types over 8 years). The R^2 = 0.62 and
  its two z scores are in the paper, not in Code S1. [RESULTS-CALIB-3.md](RESULTS-CALIB-3.md)
  shows that a significant Poisson brevity slope on these chunks is expected from wordless and
  even shuffled song.

## Prior art

Random-text arguments (Miller 1957; Li 1992) show that brevity can arise without words.
Huang et al. 2020 ("Brevity is not a universal in animal communication") show that brevity
depends on the unit of analysis. We found no published test of the whale paper's brevity
controls on sources with known truth (see [PRIOR-ART.md](PRIOR-ART.md)).
